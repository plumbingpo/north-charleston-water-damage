Attic Water Damage Help in North Charleston

Attic water damage comes from two directions. From above, rain enters through failed flashing, worn shingles, or the seals at chimneys and vents. The house itself sends moisture upward too, and in a poorly ventilated attic it condenses on cold sheathing. Either way, hardly anyone goes up there to look. In North Charleston, we inspect the attic, track down the source, and get it dry.

Most attic jobs center on wet insulation. Heating and cooling bills go up because cellulose and fiberglass alike give up R-value when wet. Worse, soaked insulation holds water against the ceiling drywall and the framing for weeks. Saturated material comes out, air movers and dehumidifiers dry the wood, and any mold on the sheathing or rafters gets treated.

Warning Signs

  • Brown rings or stains on an upstairs ceiling, often worse after rain
  • Rafters and roof decking marked with dark streaks or stains
  • Closets on the top floor, or the attic, that smell musty
  • Stains or drips where a light fixture or exhaust fan meets the ceiling
  • Bubbling or peeling ceiling paint in a room on the top floor
  • Rusty nail tips poking through the sheathing, sometimes with water beads on them
Why Speed Matters

The Damage Timeline

First storm

Insulation Takes It

Rain that gets past the roof lands in the insulation, which absorbs it. Nothing shows indoors, so the leak goes unnoticed and the roof is not repaired.

24 to 48 hours

Mold Can Start

Mold does very well on wet paper facing and damp lumber when the attic is warm and the air is still. The sheathing, along with the top face of the drywall ceiling, is where growth can begin.

A few weeks on

Stains Come Through

After repeated wetting, the insulation is saturated and water reaches the ceiling. Brown rings appear in the room below, and the drywall may start to sag.

After months

Wood Starts Rotting

Sheathing delaminates and loses its hold on the roofing nails. Large areas of roof decking are covered in mold, while rafters that never dry begin to decay.

What We Do

Our Attic Water Damage Process

1

Inspect and Find the Source

To learn whether you have a leak, condensation or the two together, we go over the roof deck, the vents, the exhaust ducts and each flashing point. How far it spread is shown by moisture meters and thermal imaging.

2

Remove Wet Insulation

Saturated or moldy insulation is bagged and removed so the wood and drywall under it can dry. Rooms under the attic are protected, and the route in and out stays contained.

3

Dry the Framing

Air movers and dehumidifiers bring the rafters, joists and sheathing back to normal moisture levels. We clean visible mold off the wood, then apply an antimicrobial.

4

Correct the Source

The roof or ventilation issue behind it is pointed out to you, and we are glad to pass what we found to your roofer. New insulation is installed to bring back the R-value after the attic is staying dry.

What You Cannot See

What Hides Behind It

Air Conditioner Drips

Many attics hold an air handler, a drain pan and condensate lines. A clogged drain or an overflowing pan drips into the insulation for weeks, with no storm to warn you.

Mold on Sheathing

With poor ventilation, mold commonly grows on the bottom of the roof deck. Before anyone looks, it may already have spread over a large section.

Misrouted Exhaust Fans

Every day, a fan duct that stops short of the roof releases humid bathroom air into the attic. On cold sheathing that air condenses, and the result is easy to mistake for a leaking roof.

A Falling Ceiling

Saturated insulation is heavy. Drywall that has been wet from above weakens at the fasteners and can drop suddenly, bringing wet insulation down with it.
