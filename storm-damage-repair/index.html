How to Prevent Storm Damage Repair Problems

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Cracked caulk lets blown rain in, so inspect and renew the seals on doors and windows
  • Keep gutters and downspouts clear so water drains properly in heavy rain
  • Runoff should flow away from your foundation, so fix low spots and add downspout extensions
  • Ice dams form when attics run warm, so check that yours has enough insulation and airflow
  • Schedule a roof inspection every year so weak spots are repaired before storm season
  • Learn where the main water shutoff is and how to close it if pipes are damaged in a storm

Why Hire a Professional for Storm Damage Repair?

Because one kind of storm damage feeds another, the work needs restoration professionals with real experience. A roof leak that looks minor from inside may have done extensive hidden damage to insulation, roof decking and wall cavities, and that leads to mold growth and structural deterioration if it is not found and dried. Professional restoration technicians use thermal imaging and moisture meters to trace water paths and identify every affected area.

Tarping and boarding up only work when they are done with the right technique. Tarps put on the wrong way may funnel water into the building or tear loose in the wind. We have done a lot of storm mitigation, and we use suitable materials and methods so the temporary protection holds until permanent repairs are on the calendar.

When to Call for Storm Damage Repair in North Charleston

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Bubbling paint and soft, drooping spots overhead once the rain stops
  • From ground level, the roof shows shingles torn off, broken or raised
  • Downspouts knocked off and gutter runs hanging after high wind
  • Metal flashing pulled away from vents, valleys or the edge of the roof
  • Damp, earthy odors that appear a few days after the storm
  • Water in the lowest level after storm drains back up or overflow
  • Water getting past doors, windows and wall penetrations when wind pushes the rain
