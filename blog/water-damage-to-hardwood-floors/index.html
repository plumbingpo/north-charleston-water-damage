Water Damage to Hardwood Floors in North Charleston, SC

Home / Blog / Saving Water-Damaged Hardwood Floors in North Charleston, SC

Hardwood floors are one of the most valuable features in any home, and they are also among the most vulnerable to water damage. Cupping, crowning, buckling, staining, and mold in the subfloor beneath are all results of water reaching hardwood. If you own hardwood in North Charleston, SC, you should know what to do when it gets wet, and how to tell floors a professional can save from floors that must be replaced.

How Water Damages Hardwood

Wood is hygroscopic, meaning it absorbs and releases moisture based on surrounding conditions. Wood fibers expand as they take on moisture, which is what happens when a floor gets too wet. The earliest sign you can see of a moisture imbalance is cupping, where board edges sit higher than the middle. Should the wetting go on, crowning may follow (the bottom dries more slowly than the top, so the center rises above the edges), or buckling (boards pull entirely free of the subfloor). When a floor stays wet a long time, mold can also develop under the boards, on the subfloor, unnoticed until they are pulled.

Can Water-Damaged Hardwood Be Saved?

Five things decide if the floor is salvageable: the speed of the professional drying response, how much water there was, the time the boards spent wet, what kind of hardwood and finish you have, and what shape the subfloor is in. Floors exposed to clean water can often be saved when professional drying starts within 24 to 48 hours. What makes it work is slow, controlled drying, so moisture leaves the wood evenly and the boards do not split or crack.

Drying Wood Floors the Professional Way

Drying hardwood is not done the way ordinary structural drying is, and it calls for its own techniques. To pull moisture evenly from the wood, mats or panels go directly on the surface and create a controlled drying environment. Floor drying systems can also be used beneath the subfloor from crawl spaces. Everything depends on an even, gradual pace, since wood dried too fast loses surface moisture before the core does and ends up permanently cupped, cracked, and split.

Signs the Floor Cannot Be Saved

As a rule, hardwood has to be replaced after more than 48 to 72 hours of being wet, after contact with contaminated water such as floodwater or sewage, when delamination or buckling is widespread, when mold is growing on the subfloor, or when the subfloor has itself been damaged.
