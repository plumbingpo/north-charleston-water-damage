Toilet Overflow Cleanup Help in North Charleston

A toilet overflow looks like a mop job, but the water does not stay on the tile. It runs under the baseboard, into the wall and through the gap around the drain pipe. The contents of that water matter more than anything. Water from the tank or supply line is clean. Bowl water is not clean, so we handle it as contaminated from the start.

In North Charleston, we begin each overflow job by determining the category of water, which decides how the rest is done. If it is clean, we extract it and dry the area. For gray or black water we wear protective gear, remove soaked porous items such as carpet pad, and disinfect each surface the water reached. Finally we meter the subfloor, the bottoms of the walls and the space underneath, so no moisture gets left behind.

First Five Minutes

Before We Arrive

  1. Shut Off the SupplyFind the shutoff valve behind the toilet and turn it clockwise as far as it goes. When the valve is frozen, remove the lid of the tank and hold the float up so filling stops.
  2. Stop FlushingHold off on flushing, and keep the washer, showers and sinks off too. With a blocked drain line, whatever goes down a drain may return through the toilet.
  3. Stay Out of Contaminated WaterBowl water, or any water with waste in it, means kids and pets stay out of the room. Should you have to cross it, wear gloves and waterproof footwear.
  4. Look at the Ceiling UnderneathIf the toilet is upstairs, go check the ceiling directly below it. Should you see drips or a sag, cut the power to the room and get belongings out from under it.
  5. Contain Clean WaterUse old towels to keep clean water from reaching carpet or hallways. Contaminated water should be left to a crew that has the right disinfectants and equipment.

Will Insurance Pay?

Insurance and Toilet Overflow Cleanup

A sudden and accidental overflow, for example from an unexpected clog or a fill valve that failed, is covered by many homeowners policies. Unless a backup endorsement was added, policies often exclude water backing up out of a drain or the sewer, which may include an overflow that a blocked main line caused. Damage from a toilet that leaked at the base for months is usually not covered. To learn which of these applies in your case, check the policy or phone your agent.

The Usual Culprits

Why This Happens

  • Septic systems that are full or drain fields that are waterlogged, which send water back toward the house.
  • Supply lines that give out or tanks that crack, releasing clean water across the floor.
  • Blocked traps and drain lines, which leave the bowl nowhere to empty but onto the floor.
  • Water escaping at the base with every flush because the wax ring has worn out.
  • Waste rising out of the lowest toilet in the house because the main sewer line is blocked.
What Is at Risk

What Happens if You Wait

Ceiling Below

From a second floor bathroom, water follows the drain pipe down and pools on the ceiling drywall beneath, leaving stains, sagging and sometimes a collapse.

Wet Vanity and Wall Bases

Drywall edges and the vanity base stand in the water and draw it up like a wick. Paint may look normal even when the wall is damp several inches up from the floor.

Swollen Baseboards and Trim

Sitting at floor level, door casings and baseboards wick water up from their lower edge. Fiberboard trim, the most common kind, swells and splits and never returns to shape.

Lingering Odor

An odor can return every time the weather turns humid if contaminated water dried beneath the floor or inside the bottom of a wall, and only removing the affected material ends it.
