A sewage backup is one of the most hazardous situations a property owner can face. Pathogens in raw sewage include salmonella, giardia, hepatitis and E. coli, along with other viruses, parasites and bacteria, and contact with anything contaminated is a serious risk to health. Across North Charleston, North Charleston Restoration answers sewage emergencies 24/7, sending trained technicians who arrive with EPA-registered disinfectants and the right protective equipment so your property is decontaminated safely.

Sewage damage is classified as Category 3 (black water), the most contaminated and dangerous category of water damage. Because of it, cleanup follows the toughest rules in the trade, which means full protective gear, barriers to contain the area, tearing out any porous material that met sewage and disinfecting all that is left. For Category 3 water damage the reference is IICRC S500, and our crews follow it to bring the building back to safe occupancy.

We clear the standing sewage first, then pull contaminated water out of structural materials, remove carpet, padding, insulation, drywall and any other porous item that was in contact with sewage for proper disposal, disinfect each hard surface, treat with antimicrobial products and finish with structural drying, which prevents secondary mold. To finish, thermal foggers and hydroxyl generators of industrial grade deodorize the space so the sewage smell does not linger.

Common Sewage Cleanup Questions

Most backups start with a blockage in the home's sewer line. Roots from nearby trees, flushed wipes and accumulated grease account for a large share of those clogs. Other possibilities are a failed old pipe and a municipal sewer overloaded by heavy rain, which sends sewage up through floor drains. A camera run through the line after cleanup identifies the actual cause.
In most cases our advice is to relocate temporarily while the cleanup happens, above all when contamination is widespread. Airborne pathogens and the chemicals used for decontamination can both pose health risks. After seeing your situation firsthand, our team tells you what makes sense.
Sewage cleanup costs in North Charleston typically range from $2,000 to $10,000 or more, depending on the extent of contamination and the area affected. Expect roughly $1,500 to $3,000 when the backup is minor and contained, and more than $15,000 in cases of extensive flooding that need materials replaced and rebuilding done.
It depends on the material. Hard, non-porous items such as metal, glass, sealed tile and solid plastic can usually be cleaned and disinfected. Upholstery, mattresses, carpet with its pad, insulation and drywall soak up sewage and cannot be made safe, so they are thrown out. For insurance purposes, our crew keeps a list of everything that had to be removed.
Keep people and pets out of the affected area. Do not flush, run faucets or start the washer or dishwasher, because added water feeds the backup. Turn off power and the HVAC system to the affected area only if you can do so without touching contaminated water. Do not try to clean it yourself, and wash thoroughly if you touched anything.

How to Prevent Sewage Cleanup Problems

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Keep cooking oil, grease and food scraps out of the drain, where they build into clogs
  • Use drain strainers in showers and sinks to catch hair and debris
  • Stormwater from a sump or downspout should never drain into the sewer, since the extra volume invites a backup
  • Every 2 to 3 years, get a sewer camera run through the line to spot developing issues
  • Flush nothing but toilet paper, because wipes, even 'flushable' ones, cause clogs
  • Roots from trees close to the sewer line work their way into pipes and block them, so have them cleared

How to Tell You Need Sewage Cleanup

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Drain openings or basement slabs coated with visible sewage residue
  • Carpet or flooring contaminated when a toilet overflowed with solid waste
  • Running a sink or washer and hearing a toilet or nearby drain gurgle
  • Tubs, toilets or floor drains pushing black water back into the room
  • Every sink, tub and shower draining slowly even after you plunge them
  • Soggy, smelly ground over the sewer line or leakage at the cleanout cap
  • Unexplained illness or gastrointestinal symptoms among household members

How We Handle Sewage Cleanup

Our systematic approach delivers quality results on every project. Here's how we handle sewage cleanup from start to finish:

1

Safety Assessment

We start by measuring how far the sewage contamination reaches, noting any safety hazards and setting up containment so clean areas are not cross-contaminated.

2

Extracting Sewage and Removing Materials

All standing sewage is extracted. Contaminated porous materials including carpet, padding, drywall and insulation are removed and properly disposed of as biohazard waste.

3

Decontamination & Sanitization

EPA-registered antimicrobials are used to disinfect what is left after a thorough cleaning of each surface. Meanwhile, air scrubbers fitted with HEPA filters pull pathogens and other contaminants from the air.

4

Drying & Odor Elimination

Commercial drying equipment removes all residual moisture. Sewage odors are eliminated through industrial deodorizing. The space is confirmed safe to occupy by clearance testing.

The Benefits of Professional Sewage Cleanup

This is one cleanup you should never take on yourself. The biological hazards in raw sewage require specialized training, proper personal protective equipment (Tyvek suits, respirators, protective gloves and boots) and EPA-registered disinfection products that are not available to consumers. Exposure to sewage pathogens can cause serious illness, including gastrointestinal disease, hepatitis, respiratory infections and skin infections.

Disposal matters as well, since everything the sewage contaminated has to be handled as biohazard waste. It is illegal to throw sewage-contaminated insulation, drywall or carpet in the regular trash, and doing so puts the community and the workers who collect it at risk. A certified restoration company follows proper disposal protocols and works with licensed facilities that accept biohazard waste.
