Wall and Drywall Water Damage Help in North Charleston

Drywall acts like a wick. Once the base of a wall gets wet, moisture climbs through the paper and gypsum, commonly a foot or more higher than the water stood on the floor. From the room, the surface may seem dry and sound even though the hidden face and the insulation are saturated. That is why we check walls with a moisture meter instead of trusting how they look.

Plenty of walls that get wet can stay. Drywall that got wet with clean water and is still firm can often be dried in place, sometimes by removing the baseboard and moving air through small holes into the cavity. When the wall is soft, crumbling, moldy or was soaked by dirty water, we make a flood cut, a straight line above the damage, and remove everything below it.

Warning Signs

  • Bubbling, wrinkled or peeling paint and wallpaper low on the wall
  • One wall noticeably cooler under your hand than the rest
  • A wall surface that gives, feels mushy or breaks apart under your thumb
  • Damp, stale odor concentrated along a single wall
  • Baseboards that swell, warp or pull away from the wall
  • New gaps along casings and baseboard tops as swollen material shifts
Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

The first few hours

Wicking Begins

Water at the base of the wall soaks into the drywall edge and starts to climb. Baseboards and the bottom plate take on moisture.

24 to 48 hours

Wall Swells

You may see blistered paint and swollen trim as the damp area moves up the wall. Growth is able to get going on the paper that lines the hidden side.

Days 3 through 7

Drywall Breaks Down

Gypsum softens and the paper lets go. A musty odor grows stronger while the batts settle and mold covers more of the unseen face.

Past one week

Damage Reaches the Studs

Studs and plates stay wet long enough to grow mold and begin to decay. A far larger section of wall needs tearing out and rebuilding.

What You Cannot See

The Damage You Do Not See

Mold Inside Cavities

The back of the drywall is dark, still and lined with paper, which is ideal for mold. That side may be coated in growth even as the room side appears spotless.

Insulation Holding Water

Batts of fiberglass absorb water and keep it pressed to the framing. No amount of airflow on the painted side will dry a cavity packed with wet batts.

Water Next Door

Water passes under the bottom plate into the next room or closet. Every wall involved is tested on its two faces, and so is the adjacent flooring.

Wet Outlets and Wiring

Water pools at the same height as most receptacles and cable runs. Leave the outlets on a damp wall alone until someone has checked the circuit.

Repair or Replace

Dry It or Replace It?

It depends on the material, how long it stayed wet and how clean the water was. We test before we decide, and we tell you why.

What We Do

How We Handle Wall and Drywall Water Damage

1

Find Every Wet Area

Every wall is checked with a thermal camera and moisture meter, and we mark the height and reach of the water, covering the other face and the rooms next door.

2

Decide What Stays

Based on the water category, how long it sat and how the drywall feels, we tell you plainly which sections can be dried in place and which need to come out.

3

Open Up or Air Out

Where the drywall stays, the trim is pulled and airflow is sent inside the wall. For walls being removed, we make a level flood cut and take out wet insulation.

4

Dry and Rebuild

Dehumidifiers and air movers stay on, usually 3 to 5 days, until the studs and the drywall left in place test dry. After that comes insulation, new drywall hung and finished, trim put back and paint.
