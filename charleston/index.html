How Charleston Homeowners Can Avoid Water Damage

A flood in the house feels like it came from nowhere, yet most trace back to wear that built up over months or years. For homeowners in Charleston, prevention is mainly a matter of knowing where to look.

Plumbing comes first. A water bill that climbs for no reason, or pressure that seems too high, can signal a concealed leak or strain on the system. Next come appliances. What Usually Goes Wrong in Charleston Homes

Water trouble is not all equally urgent, and knowing the difference helps you decide what to do first. The five below are ones we see constantly in Charleston, listed from most pressing to least.

Bursts: A pipe that has split is the most urgent call there is, because it puts out water for as long as the supply is on. Depending on the season, the break may follow a freeze, and it often shows only when the ice inside melts. Shut the house supply, run a tap to drain the lines and phone us immediately, whatever the hour. Ceilings that bulge with trapped water should be left alone and the room below kept empty.

Runoff: Storm water coming under a door or through a garage is next in line. You cannot stop the rain, but you can move belongings up and keep away from any outlets near the water. Having passed over pavement and soil on the way in, it carries dirt and germs. The North Charleston Restoration team extracts it once conditions are safe, then removes soaked materials that cannot be cleaned.

Kitchens: Leaks from a dishwasher, an ice maker line or the plumbing under the kitchen sink should be dealt with the day you find them. Close the small valve under the sink or behind the appliance and the flow usually stops. What remains is the water trapped below the cabinets and floor covering, and it will not dry by itself. In Charleston kitchens we often dry cabinets in place by sending air through small holes in the toe kick.

Crawlspaces: Dampness in the space below the floor can wait a few days, though not a few months. When we look underneath, we tend to find water lying on the liner, insulation that is soaked or sweating ducts. Given time, that humidity works its way upstairs and the joists start to rot. Our crew clears any standing water, brings the framing down to a normal moisture level and reports the likely cause.

Odors: Last on the list is a stale, earthy odor in a room that once had a leak, which is worth a scheduled visit. Since growth can start 24 to 48 hours after materials get wet, an old leak that was never fully dried is a likely culprit. Do not scrub or spray a large patch yourself, because disturbing it spreads spores through the house. We check for hidden dampness, set up containment and clear out the affected material for homeowners in Charleston.

What to Do Before Our Charleston Crew Arrives

What you should do before we arrive depends on what kind of water you are looking at. Water from a broken supply line or an overflowing sink is the safest kind. It is fine to step through in footwear, towel it up and carry your things clear. Discharge from appliances, such as a dishwasher or washer, carries more bacteria, and gloves and hand washing are sensible. Sewage and floodwater from outdoors are a health hazard, and the only job for you is to close the door and keep everyone out.

In every case, there are simple ways to save the crew time when it reaches Charleston. Make room for the truck near the entrance and clear the route from the front door to the damage. Note the time you discovered the problem and your best guess at the source. Have your insurance details at hand if you can reach them without trouble.

Time matters because clean water does not stay clean. After it has sat in building materials it degrades, and mold can take hold in 24 to 48 hours. So even for a leak in Charleston that looks minor, the first hour is the one to use well.
