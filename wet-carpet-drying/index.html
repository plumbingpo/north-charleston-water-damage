Wet Carpet Drying: What North Charleston Homeowners Should Know

Carpet holds far more water than it looks like it does. Often the pad is full of water and the subfloor is wet beneath it, even when the fibers you touch feel only slightly damp. Towels and a shop vacuum reach the top layer and little else. In North Charleston, we pull the water out, take moisture meter readings on each layer, and place drying equipment that works down to the subfloor.

Whether the carpet can be saved depends on two things, how clean the water was and how long it has been wet. Carpet soaked by a clean supply line can usually be dried in place if we get to it quickly. Water from an appliance counts as gray, so the pad is frequently removed while the carpet is cleaned and treated. After sewage or flood water from outdoors, neither the carpet nor the pad stays.

What It Looks Like

  • The damp area gets wider over time with no new spill to explain it
  • Paint blistering near the floor, or baseboard trim that has puffed up
  • Backing that separates from the carpet when you lift a corner to look
  • Dark, rusty lines along the wall where the tack strip has gotten wet
  • Ripples or loose spots where the carpet has let go of the tack strip
  • A clammy, cool patch underfoot that lingers well past the original spill
Behind the Surface

The Damage You Do Not See

Mold Under the Carpet

Growth usually starts on the underside of the carpet, where it is dark and damp. The odor arrives a long time ahead of any visible sign on top.

Damp Base of the Walls

Water that runs under the carpet reaches the base of the walls. Because carpet covers the edge, no one sees the drywall and baseboards drawing the water upward.

Stains From Furniture

When carpet is wet, the finish on wooden legs and the rust from metal glides transfer into it. Such spots are frequently there for good, while the legs soak up water and swell.

Swollen Subfloor

Left wet, the OSB or particleboard beneath a carpet swells and then crumbles. Weeks later, with the spill long forgotten, you get raised seams, squeaks and soft spots.

What We Do

Our Wet Carpet Drying Process

1

Map the Wet Area

Our first job is finding the outer limit of the water, under walls and in closets too, using a thermal camera and moisture meters. We also identify the water category, because that decides what can stay.

2

Pull Out the Water

Weighted extraction tools press water out of the carpet and the pad together. This removes far more than a wand alone, and it shortens the drying time that follows.

3

Dry Every Layer

Depending on the job, we float the carpet on moving air or lift it and remove the pad. Dehumidifiers and air movers stay on until the subfloor and the carpet each test dry.

4

Clean and Reinstall

Where needed, an antimicrobial goes on the subfloor and carpet, then new pad is laid if the old was removed, and the carpet is stretched back on its tack strip and cleaned.

The Clock

What Happens if You Wait

The first few hours

Water Moves Outward

Water travels through the pad far beyond the patch you can see. The tack strip starts taking on water as it creeps below the walls toward the room next door.

24 to 48 hours

Smell and Mold Start

That sour wet carpet odor shows up as bacteria multiply in the damp pad. Mold can begin growing on the backing and on dust trapped deep in the fibers.

Days 3 through 7

The Carpet Breaks Down

As the latex bonding the backing gives out, the carpet delaminates and starts to ripple. Nails in the tack strip start rusting, and the subfloor swells.

Past one week

Too Late to Save

By now the carpet and pad usually have to come out. Often mold has spread to the subfloor and the base of the drywall, which adds to the job.
